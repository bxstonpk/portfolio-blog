I didn't start out writing code. I started in accounting, reconciling numbers and closing books. Here's how that turned into building software.

## Where I Started

Cost and financial analysis taught me something I didn't expect: how much time gets lost to manual, repetitive work. Every month-end close had the same tedious spreadsheet steps.

## The Turn

I started automating small parts of my own job — first with spreadsheet macros, then scripts. That curiosity turned into a move toward business analysis, where I got to formally bridge "what the business needs" with "what a system can do."

## Becoming a Developer

Once I understood the *what*, I wanted to build the *how* myself instead of only specifying it for someone else. I picked up full-stack development, and my accounting and analysis background turned out to be an advantage — I already understood the business problems I was now building solutions for.

## What I'd Tell Someone Earlier in the Path

- Domain knowledge is not wasted time — it's leverage once you can also build
- Start automating whatever's tedious in your current job; that instinct transfers directly
- You don't need a traditional path to become a developer

## Now

I get to work across the whole chain — understanding the business problem, designing the solution, and building it. That's exactly where I wanted to end up.
